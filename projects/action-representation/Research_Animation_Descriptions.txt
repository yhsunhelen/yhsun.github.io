ACTION REPRESENTATION FOR SPATIAL GENERALIZATION IN ROBOT WORLD MODELS
Homepage animations — concise scientific explanations

MAIN TITLE
Where a cut happens is not what makes it different.

HERO SUMMARY
Two synchronized blade actions reveal what a robot world model should recognize as equivalent, and what it must keep distinct. Moving fruit and blade together changes their world coordinates without changing the fruit-relative cut. Keeping the blade motion fixed while changing the peel thickness can expose flesh in one case and leave peel in another. A useful action representation should reuse the same physical interaction across space without erasing the state that determines the result.

VIDEO A — Two world poses. One physical cut.
The same recorded blade stroke is applied to the same fruit under two shared rigid poses. The paths look different in world coordinates, yet every blade–fruit relation and the resulting material-removal field remain identical in fruit coordinates. A five-seed prediction comparison at the largest tested pose range gives NRMSE 1.002 for world-relative and 0.858 for body-relative inputs (Figure 3a).

VIDEO B — Same blade action. Different material outcomes.
The blade follows an identical 40-point contact trajectory with the same removal-depth field on the same fruit geometry. Only the peel-thickness field changes. At one sampled mesh position, q = 1.7214 mm, thin peel h = 1.3616 mm, and thick peel h = 2.0424 mm. The first cut enters the flesh by 0.3598 mm, while the second leaves 0.3210 mm of peel. These are actual values from the frozen numerical oracle data rather than constructed demonstration numbers. The distinct prediction error for full versus thickness-blind input (NRMSE 0.3888 vs 0.8596 in Figure 4a) is a separate five-seed test showing why the thickness variable matters.

FULL VIDEO — Conclusion
Action coordinates matter because they determine which previous experiences can be reused in new spatial settings. This reuse is justified when the physical interaction is equivalent, but it cannot replace missing material information. Furthermore, even if the physics is unchanged, neural predictions may still respond to changes of the estimated coordinates. The conclusion concerns the joint state–action input and the trained predictor, not a claim that one coordinate frame is always optimal.
